AC inverter generator or DC charging generator?

Choose AC when the load expects AC at a specified voltage and frequency. Choose a DC configuration only after matching the receiving equipment, charge controls and voltage range. The same power number can describe two incompatible outputs.

Start at the receiving connector

For an appliance, photograph the rating plate and identify the plug and frequency. For a battery system, identify the pack model and the charger or controller between the supply and battery. A nominal bus voltage by itself cannot establish that the battery can accept a generator output.

Compare two 1.8 kW entries

HW2000iS-B2/C/C2 appears in the AC inverter table at 1.8 kW rated and 2.0 kW maximum. HW2750CZ-K/M/L carries those same power ratings in the DC table. The first line calls for an AC outlet configuration. The second calls for an ordered DC voltage and confirmation of the receiving controller. Substituting one on wattage alone would change the interface.

Make configuration part of the order

List model suffix, continuous demand, voltage, connector, start method and the expected behavior when a load starts or the battery becomes full. For a DC project, request documentation from both equipment suppliers showing that their control and current limits are compatible. For an AC project, request the exact voltage/frequency nameplate.

Allow for exhaust in the site plan

Fuel-powered portable generators produce carbon monoxide. US CPSC guidance calls for outdoor operation at least 20 feet from a home, directing exhaust away. For a project involving occupied vehicles, obtain the manufacturer’s operating and placement instructions before selecting a location.

Safety source: US Consumer Product Safety Commission.
