What to check before powering a car fridge overnight

Measure energy used over a representative night and check supply compatibility separately. A fridge’s running wattage, battery nameplate capacity and an ECO-mode label are each insufficient to predict overnight runtime.

Define the night you need to cover

Record the planned hours, starting temperature of the contents, ambient temperature, setpoint and how often the lid opens. Measurements from an already cold fridge can differ from an evening that begins with warm contents. Use the same conditions when comparing models.

Read energy at the power source

Use a meter suited to the approved supply and connector. Record total Wh for the whole test, including compressor-off periods. For example, 180 Wh over a nine-hour test is a 20 W average. This is a worked example, independent of the 13 L or 18 L product specifications. Keep the meter reading and start/end times with the result.

Compare with the available battery budget

Use the energy actually available at the selected outlet after the reserve and conversion losses. If that budget is 300 Wh, a measured 180 Wh test would leave 120 Wh under those same conditions. Separately verify startup current, voltage range and low-voltage cutoff against the appliance and supply manuals.

What to ask when ordering the fridge

Request the exact DC input range, connector, protection settings and a consumption test with specified conditions. For the 18 L glass-panel model, ask which control mode was used. If a fleet needs a guaranteed reserve, validate the sample with the actual auxiliary power arrangement before setting an overnight operating policy.
