Power and vehicle refrigeration buying guides

Find worked examples and model comparisons for home batteries, portable power stations, generators and vehicle refrigerators.

Energy storage

Home battery kWh vs kW: which number sizes backup?

Use kWh to estimate how long a system can supply energy. Use kW to check whether the inverter can carry the appliances that run together. A backup design needs both numbers.

What can a 550 Wh portable power station run?

For the documented 550 Wh station, check the outlet first. Its AC inverter is rated at 500 W with a 220 V, 50 Hz output. The DC outlet has a separate 12 V, 10 A limit. Battery capacity helps estimate duration after the load passes those checks.

Generators

AC inverter generator or DC charging generator?

Choose AC when the load expects AC at a specified voltage and frequency. Choose a DC configuration only after matching the receiving equipment, charge controls and voltage range. The same power number can describe two incompatible outputs.

Rated watts vs peak watts on an inverter generator

Size sustained loads against rated output. Maximum output is a separate short-duration figure. Check the permitted duration and the appliance starting demand before counting on that extra capacity.

Vehicle refrigerators

13 L or 18 L car refrigerator: which fits your vehicle?

The documented 13 L cabinet is narrower, while both documented 18 L variants are lower. Use the available width, height and lid clearance to decide. Liters describe internal capacity and cannot establish vehicle fit.

What to check before powering a car fridge overnight

Measure energy used over a representative night and check supply compatibility separately. A fridge’s running wattage, battery nameplate capacity and an ECO-mode label are each insufficient to predict overnight runtime.
